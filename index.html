How this started

Three weeks ago, we asked 1,062 of our customers one question.

Not “rate your order.” We asked: what do you actually want to know about sex that nobody will tell you?

They sent back 738 questions that had clearly been waiting years for a safe place to be asked:

“Why does orgasm take longer and longer — and why will nobody explain it?”— survey respondent, 55+
“Is my antidepressant the reason I can’t finish?”— survey respondent, 45–54
“I’m on HRT and got my libido back. But it doesn’t help the brain. What helps the brain?”— survey respondent, 45–54
“Is sex over for me?”— survey respondent, 55+
“Why do I need to ask an expert? Why can’t I find out these things by myself?”— the question that became this program

She can’t. We checked what’s out there. Medical pages that describe your body like a diagnosis. Content that’s basically porn. Advice written for 25-year-olds, about bodies that behave like 25-year-old bodies.

4 in 10 of the women we surveyed said they can’t tell what’s credible and what’s nonsense. 1 in 5 have never dared to search at all.

So sex education just… stops. One embarrassed unit of it at fifteen, built entirely around preventing things. Then the curriculum ends — right before the decades where your body changes the most, the questions get genuinely hard, and the answers genuinely exist.

This is the second curriculum. The one that should have existed all along.
